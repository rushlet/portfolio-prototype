FLOURISH · CHART ENGINEERING

Making room for the data

I led the design and engineering of external labels for Flourish pie and donut charts, helping chart makers keep categories readable when slices are small or space is limited.

When the text outgrows the slice

A label inside a slice can disappear as soon as the slice gets too small. Moving it outside makes room for the text and raises another question. How do you keep the label near its slice when several categories are competing for the same space?

External labels in a crowded chart

Seven categories share a fictional community budget. Parks and open spaces has the largest share at 42%. The three smallest slices together make up 10%, with their labels spaced apart near the top of the two columns.

How the layout works

Labels form columns on either side of the chart. Elbow leader lines connect each label to its slice. The layout adjusts vertical spacing to reduce overlaps and can wrap or truncate long labels. When space runs out, it hides labels it cannot place while favouring placements close to their segments.

Segment count, text length, font size and available space all affect what fits. A legend can still give readers a way to identify categories whose labels are hidden.

Designing for more than one chart

I worked with product designers through review and a substantial rework. We needed a default chart makers could override, with behaviour that held up when the chart resized, appeared in a grid or transitioned between label states.

Several small slices on the same side can fill one column even when the other has room. A zero-value category also needs consideration because it has no visible slice for a leader line to point to.

Shipping a new default

We released external labels as the default for new charts in October 2026. A new major template version let existing charts keep their appearance. The team also updated starter examples to show the new default.
