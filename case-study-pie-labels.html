FLOURISH · CHART ENGINEERING

Responsive labels for pie and donut charts

A slice can be big enough to see and still be too small to label. I led the design and engineering of external labels for Flourish’s pie and donut charts, so readers could match labels to small slices even in a grid of charts.

My roleFeature lead, working with product designers
Built withD3 and SVG
ReleasedOctober 2026

Labels had to fit inside the slice

Before this release, labels inside the slices were the only placement option in Flourish pie and donut charts. When a slice was too small to hold its label, readers had to look elsewhere for the category name. External labels gave those names room outside the pie, with leader lines showing which slice each label belonged to.

How the layout works

Labels form columns on either side of the chart. Elbow leader lines connect each label to its slice. The layout adjusts vertical spacing to reduce overlaps and can wrap or truncate long labels. When space runs out, it hides labels it cannot place while favouring placements close to their segments.

I also updated the legend’s Auto setting to respond to what the labels show. Auto can hide the legend when labels already show category names and values, and shows it with value-only labels so readers can identify the categories. Auto also considers how many slices there are, how long the labels are, the font size and the space available when deciding whether a legend is needed.

Designing for more than one chart

I worked with product designers to make the layout respond as a chart resized or appeared in a grid. I also handled the animated transitions between label states.

I fixed cases where labels clustered on one side, where a label had zero width and where a category had a value of zero. A zero-value category has no visible slice for a leader line to point to.

Shipping a new default

In October 2026, we shipped the long-requested feature as the default for new charts and kept internal labels available as an option. We released it in a new major template version so existing charts kept their appearance. I worked with a designer to update several starter examples so chart makers could see the new default as soon as they tried the template.
